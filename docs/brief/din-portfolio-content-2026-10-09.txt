 Rijad Kuloglija
 Lead Software Developer / Full-Stack
 Building software professionally since 2021
 Rijad is Lead Software Developer on the LibLynx development team — embedded through Clearview, a remote-first consultancy. He joined as a Senior Frontend Developer in July 2025 and was promoted to lead three months later; he now leads the build of the platform's report-builder application: drag-and-drop report composition over COUNTER usage data, in React 19, TypeScript and Material UI. Before that he owned design-to-deployment development of internal banking products at ProCredit Bank, and spent two years at Bloola in Germany on projects for BMW and Deutsche Bundesbank.
 He is also CTO & Head of Software Development at DIN Solution, an international FinTech consulting firm, and one of the four members of Neo Labs, an EU AI-voice company, as Head of Communications & Software Innovation — where he engineered the customer-facing platforms of its products Neo Care and Neo Therapy. His center of gravity is frontend architecture — React, Vue 3, TypeScript, Next.js — backed by real backend work in Laravel, Flask and Node; along the way he has integrated AI into a Swiss legal-tech product and shipped a decentralized application on the Sui blockchain.

 03 — Why us
 Where we add leverage.
 01
 Product ownership
 We don't need perfectly prepared tickets to make progress. We can take a product problem and turn it into architecture, implementation and production software.
 02
 Across the stack
 Frontend, backend, data, APIs, infrastructure and deployment. We understand the consequences of decisions across system boundaries.

 Existing systems
 We're comfortable entering mature codebases, understanding the architecture that's already there, and improving systems without rebuilding everything from scratch.

 Shipping
 The goal is production. Architecture and code quality matter because software needs to survive after launch.

 06 — Principles
 How we build.
 01
 Understand the product before choosing the architecture.
 02
 Prefer simple systems that can evolve over clever systems nobody wants to maintain.
 03
 Measure performance instead of guessing.
 04
 Treat production as part of development.
 05
 Design APIs and interfaces around real workflows.
 06
 Ship, observe, improve.
